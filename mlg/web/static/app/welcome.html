How it works

Not a photo tagger: every step keeps its evidence, and the crew checks its own work.

  1. Add your photos

    Point it at a folder or your Facebook or Instagram download. Files are read where they are; copies are set aside by visual fingerprint.

  2. Muse understands them

    Who is there, what happened, and when, with the reason. A name goes on a face only when two independent signals agree.

  3. Ask for a film

    "A film of my daughter growing up." The crew researches, writes, asks you what only you know, edits, scores and narrates.

  4. It watches and re-cuts

    A critic watches the draft and leaves timed notes. The editor re-cuts, and a blind test picks the better version.

Run it on your own photos

Your family's photos never have to leave home. The app runs on your computer and reads your photos in place.

Download for Windows Source code

Unzip and open MuseLifeGraph.exe. The app isn't code-signed yet: if Windows warns you, choose More info, then Run anyway. macOS and Linux: run from source, see GitHub.

  • Windows 10 or 11. The first screen walks you through setup and tests each key before saving it.
  • A Meta Model API key from dev.meta.ai. About $0.006 a photo, paid to Meta directly; the app shows the estimate before it spends anything.
  • A free graph database (Neo4j AuraDB Free, a few minutes to create).
  • Optional: a Baseten key to run search and checks on Muse Spark Open, and an NVIDIA GPU for music and narration. Without a GPU, films get captions.
