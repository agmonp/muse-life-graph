# Evals (2026-10-31 17:34)

Measured on the owner's real library (3,080 photos and videos of one family, shared with consent) and on every finished film of the hosted demo. Generated by `scripts/evals.py`; raw numbers in `data/cache/evals.json`. Samples are small: read these as honest spot checks, not benchmarks.

## 1. Who is in the photo: face fingerprints vs the earlier Life Graph

Truth: the owner's earlier Life Graph says who is in each photo (not where). Test: 3,508 photos, 10,186 faces with a fingerprint, 7,091 (photo, person) pairs for the 378 people who have reference faces. Fingerprints (YuNet + SFace) are matched against reference faces only, never against faces already linked, and 23 faces that are themselves a reference picture are left out. A "wrong" can also be a person the earlier graph missed.

| rule | precision | recall | right | wrong | missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| similarity ≥ 0.40 | 68% | 56% | 3,969 | 1,901 | 3,122 |
| similarity ≥ 0.50 | 78% | 41% | 2,928 | 803 | 4,163 |
| similarity ≥ 0.50, 0.10 ahead of the next person | 80% | 37% | 2,619 | 667 | 4,472 |
| similarity ≥ 0.62, 0.10 ahead of the next person | 80% | 13% | 939 | 229 | 6,152 |

The app links a face only when two keys agree (fingerprint ≥ 0.50 and the earlier graph saw the person in that photo, or ≥ 0.62 alone with a 0.10 margin), so its own links on these photos are 93% precise with 60% recall (4,222 right, 330 not in the earlier graph). That number leans on the earlier graph and is not an independent test; the table above is. Of all 7,091 pairs, 706 can never be matched: the photo has fewer usable faces than the people the earlier graph lists (turned away, too small, not detected). Every other miss is a face that was found but scored below the bar.

On the "Who is this?" screen the owner answered 38 faces by hand (no machine guess was stored for those faces, so they are not scored here).

## 2. Face groups ("Who is this?")

Unnamed faces are grouped so that one answer names many. On the 4,694 faces whose person is known (98 people), the same grouping rule makes 108 groups of 2+ faces: **97% of their faces belong to the group's main person** and 69% of the groups are a single person. The rule splits rather than merges: a person spans a median of 1 group(s), and 85 faces stay alone. Most of these faces were linked with the help of the same fingerprints, so this is an upper bound; the check by eye below is on faces nobody has named.

Checked by eye (the coding agent (Claude), by eye on 160 px person crops, not the owner, 2026-10-31): 12 random open groups of 3+ faces, 4 one person, 7 mixed, 1 unsure. The mixed ones are mostly small or dark faces, and one 136-face group is a catch-all of hands, backs and blurred crops where no face is really visible. A face crop is small, so some 'mixed' calls may be wrong either way; the owner has not checked these.

## 4. Does the narration match the picture?

A separate Muse Spark 1.3 judge looked at the first picture under each of 35 narration lines in the 8 films:

| match | partly | no |
|---|---|---|
| 40% | 51% | 9% |

The Picture Checker rewrote 5 lines: the screenwriter's version matched its picture 20% of the time, the rewrite 20%. The judge is the same model family as the checker with a different prompt: a friendly judge.

**A bug this measurement found.** All 8 films were made while the Picture Checker's rejections were silently
ignored: the models answer with picture ids missing their `:moment` ending, so none of the 20 pictures the
checker wanted out of these films was removed (its narration rewrites did apply). Fixed on 2026-10-31
(`checker._match_id`); films made from now on drop those pictures, so this table is the before.

## 6. Muse Spark Open on Baseten vs the Muse Spark API

`scripts/eval_spark_open.py`, 2026-10-31 17:45: the same 30 inputs, prompts, schemas and effort, uncached: 15 search questions (English and Hebrew) turned into people, years and search words, and 15 Picture Checker scenes from the demo's films (35 pictures). API = `muse-spark-1.3`, Spark Open = `meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026`.

| | Muse Spark API | Muse Spark Open (Baseten) |
|---|---|---|
| answered | 30/30 | 30/30 |
| median latency | 4.94 s | 7.07 s |
| output tokens (of which reasoning) | 35,858 (31,458) | 33,002 (26,301) |
| cost of the 30 calls (list price, same per token) | $0.477 | $0.464 |

**Search understanding:** the same people, years and kinds on 10/15 questions (people 11/15, years 14/15); search words overlap 25% (Jaccard).
**Picture Checker:** the same keep/drop on 33/35 pictures (14/15 scenes fully agree), the same person framed on 24/35, the same narration verdict on 15/15 scenes.

**What differs.** On search, Spark Open reads people more literally than the API: "the kids" became the owner
plus nine children, "grandma" became the two grandmothers, "we" in a Hebrew question became the owner, and
"Boaz" matched both people of that name. That is arguably closer to the question, though it narrows results.
One real miss: it dropped the year from "Who came to the wedding in 2017?". Search words overlap only a
quarter of the time because both models invent their own synonyms; the people and years drive the filters.
On the Picture Checker the two models nearly always make the same keep/drop call and agree on every
narration verdict; they frame a different person in about a third of the pictures (usually two people of
equal weight in the shot).

**Latency and cost.** Text calls are about as fast as the API (search median 3.7 s vs 3.8 s). Calls with
pictures are slower and much less predictable on Baseten: median 15.4 s vs 6.1 s, and four of 15 took over
3 minutes (the longest 314 s). The list price per token is the same, and the 30 calls cost the same within
3%. That is why the Picture Checker runs scenes in parallel and falls back to the API on an error, and why
perception of the whole library stays on the API.
